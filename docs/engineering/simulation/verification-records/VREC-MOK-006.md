## What this record does not claim

reader.** That is the single largest limitation on this record and it is not mitigated by their being
thorough. Seven found nothing adverse and one is **qualified adverse**; an agent assessing its own
change is the wrong party to be reassured by, and `manual-assessment.md` is offered as a record of what
was read and concluded, not as independent review.

`VER-MOK-005`'s seven manual assessments remain outstanding and this record does not close them. This
work relocated 16 of that suite's cross-cutting tests to `mokiterions-tui/tests/verification.rs` and
left 8 inline; the tests are byte-identical either way, so the relocation neither performs nor
invalidates those assessments. Everything `VREC-MOK-005` said about them still stands, including that
the observer cannot be driven from this environment.

Three further limits, stated because they bound what a green gate means here:

- **There is no compile-fail harness.** That a public-tier test *cannot* reach a private item is
  established by the compiler refusing to link one — real, and real only for tests that were written.
  It is not a mechanical proof that a future violating test fails to compile.
- **Equivalence is demonstrated across the declared matrices, not the input space.** Neither a
  directory move nor a library target has a plausible mechanism for input-dependent divergence, but
  that is an argument, not a measurement.
- **The initiative's central benefit is not measured.** That a break in what the observer presents now
  fails a test with no private access to repair itself with would require deliberately breaking the
  contract to demonstrate. What is verified is the precondition for it: 77 tests reach the observer only
  through its public interface.

Nothing here bears on the correctness of the simulation, which is `VER-MOK-001` through `VER-MOK-005`'s
subject and which this work order was written specifically not to touch.
