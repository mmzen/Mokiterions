Why this check exists
---------------------

`EventDetail::AgentInitialized` gains a field, and its rendering puts that field first:

    name:Nurb,position:62:104,territory:B,health:100,satiety:100,energy:100,fear:0,waste_tolerance:40

Two engine test files read that stream by its rendered text rather than by parsing keys into a map,
which is what VER-MOK-011 calls parsing it "positionally" —
`mokiterions-core/tests/process.rs` and `mokiterions-core/tests/viability.rs`. If either had had to
be adjusted to accommodate the new field, the record's shape would have changed for every existing
consumer, and REQ-MOK-040's additivity claim would have been about the draws only. The check is
therefore not "do they still pass" but "are they the same bytes": passing after an edit would prove
nothing about a consumer that this repository does not contain.

Both files are byte-identical to the baseline. Neither was opened.

What makes this possible
------------------------

Neither parser indexes fields by ordinal. Both anchor on the rendering's tail, which is the part
the new field cannot move:

    tests/process.rs:116-129   counts the substrings "event=agent_initialized",
                               "waste_tolerance:" and ",fear:0,waste_tolerance:", the last being
                               the pairing that makes it an initialization assertion
    tests/viability.rs:40-49   filters on "event=agent_initialized" and reads the trait with
                               `line.rsplit_once(",waste_tolerance:")`

Inserting `name:` ahead of `position:` changes none of those substrings and moves nothing to the
right of `fear`, so both files hold without an edit. That is the reason the field is first rather
than appended: SPEC-MOK-001's amendment records it as a decision, and this file is the measurement
that the decision holds. `waste_tolerance` stays last, which
`mokiterions-core/tests/naming.rs::the_initialization_record_keeps_the_name_first_and_the_trait_last`
asserts as a contract rather than an accident.

Twelve records per run grow by 118 bytes in total — 46 characters of name plus the twelve `name:`
keys and their commas — which is the figure `additivity.txt` removes per cell before
comparing streams, and it is removed by deleting one leading field rather than by re-parsing a line.
