## Why three and not two

`SPEC-MOK-004` rule 11 records one-invocation totals, and rule 11's delegating clause says a work
order that adds a test corrects these figures here and one that loses a test has a defect. A merge
can do both at once and a two-way comparison cannot tell them apart. Against master alone the
merge looks like +34 with nothing lost; against the branch alone it looks like +52 with two lost.
Neither reading is wrong and neither is sufficient. The three-way set arithmetic is what settles
it, and it is set arithmetic over target-qualified names rather than over counts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
### The arithmetic

Writing M for master's 264 names, B for the branch's 246 and G for the merge's 298, each name
qualified by the binary that ran it:

    |M| = 264      |B| = 246
    |M ∩ B| = 210          the two chains share 210 tests, the base's inheritance
    |M ∪ B| = 300          264 + 246 - 210
    |M \ B| =  54          master brings 54 the branch never had
    |B \ M| =  36          the branch brings 36 master never had

    |G| = 298 = |M ∪ B| - 2

    G \ (M ∪ B) = ∅        the merge invents no test
    M \ G       = ∅        the merge loses nothing of master's
    B \ G       = 2        exactly two of the branch's names are absent
    G \ M       = 34       and 34 of the branch's 36 arrive: 36 - 2 = 34

Every figure above is a set difference over the three logs, not a subtraction of totals. The two
identities that carry the weight are `M \ G = ∅` and `G \ (M ∪ B) = ∅`: the first says the merge
dropped none of master's tests, the second says it fabricated none. Between them the only thing
left to account for is the two names in `B \ G`, and they are the subject of the next section but
one.

Per target, master to the merge, from `census-by-target-master-vs-merge.txt`:

    mokiterions  unittests src/lib.rs      82 ->  96   +14
    cli  tests/cli.rs                      15 ->  18    +3
    records  tests/records.rs               0 ->  17   +17
    every other target of the twenty-two   unchanged, to the test
    TOTAL                                 264 -> 298   +34

Nineteen of the twenty-two targets are unchanged to the test, one is new, and two grew. That is
rule 11's conservation clause measured against master rather than argued.

### The 54 arrivals from master's side, and why they are not listed as this merge's work

`|M \ B| = 54`. Those 54 are master's — Phase 3's social decision source, its combat resolution,
the observer's announcement and hint work, and the two renames below. They arrive in the merge
because the merge takes master, and `census-by-target-branch-vs-merge.txt` lists all 54 by name
against the branch's log. They are not repeated here: this file reconciles what the merge does,
and against master the merge adds 34 and removes none. The 54 are already reconciled in master's
own packets.

The reason both directions are retained rather than only the one that flatters the change is rule
12 and the two names below. A packet that only compared against master would show zero removals
and would never have to explain them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
### The two absences, and why neither is a loss

    absent from the merge, present on the branch:

      layout        the_log_is_ten_rows_only_where_both_thresholds_are_met
      verification  no_shipped_decision_source_has_a_proposal_rejected

Both are renames master performed, and both are renames in the precise sense `SPEC-MOK-004` rule
12 fixes: a test renamed with its assertion strengthened is not a departure and its new name is
not an arrival. The pairs, with the new name measured as present in master's log and in the
merge's, in the same binary:

    layout        the_log_is_ten_rows_only_where_both_thresholds_are_met
              ->  the_log_is_six_rows_wherever_it_is_present                    (in master: layout)

    verification  no_shipped_decision_source_has_a_proposal_rejected
              ->  no_source_confined_to_the_valid_action_list_has_a_proposal_rejected
                                                                          (in master: verification)

Neither new name is in `B`, both are in `M`, and both are in `G`. So each pair is one name leaving
and one arriving *on master's side of the merge*, and the merge inherits the result. The two
absences are therefore accounted for as master's renames, already recorded in `SPEC-MOK-004`'s own
rows, and the merge neither performed them nor lost anything by taking them.

That is the whole of `B \ G`. There is no third absence, and no absence anywhere in `M \ G`.

A note on the instrument, because it bears on how these two lines are reported. The
`census-by-target.py` retained in `analysis/` printed, unconditionally, *"There is no removal, so
no baseline test was renamed, moved between targets, or deleted"*. Against the branch-to-merge
comparison that sentence is false, and a verification instrument printing a false claim is worse
than a wrong figure in a document, because a reader has no reason to doubt it. It is a condition
now, and the no-removal branch is word for word what it was, so `analysis/census-by-target.txt`
regenerates byte for byte above its own embedded script text. `README.md` records the correction
as a finding against this packet's own instruments.
