### Declared commands that were deliberately not run, and why

$ cargo fetch --locked
Not run. It is `.github/workflows/dependency-declarations.yml`'s first step and it requires
network access to the registry; this capture is offline, which is why every cargo command above
carries `--offline`. What the step exists to prove — that the committed lockfile is complete
and resolves without amendment — is proved here instead by `--locked --offline` succeeding on
clippy, on the test run and on all three `cargo tree` forms, and by
`check_declared_dependencies.py` reporting `builds and tests offline from the committed
lockfile`. A lockfile that needed fetching would have failed those, not passed them.

$ python scripts/check_release_authorization.py --root . --tag <tag>
$ python scripts/check_release_reachability.py --root . --commit <commit>
Not run, and not applicable. Both require an argument naming a release: a tag and a commit
respectively, and both exit 2 without one. They are `release.yml`'s gates and they answer
whether a release is authorized and whether its commit is reachable on the default branch.
This merge is not a release, nothing here is tagged, and the branch is deliberately unpushed.
They are named here so that the set of repository scripts is accounted for rather than
silently partial.

### What this capture does not do

It does not verify. Every command above is derived, read-only evidence, and the harness says so
itself in the line it prints at the foot of `preflight` and `inspect`: preflight and inspection
do not approve artifacts, authorize a diff, verify work, release software, commit, push, tag,
publish or deploy. Act 4's record at the merge commit — provisionally `VREC-MOK-019`, the
identifier having moved twice under `README.md` finding 10 — is where the accountable decision is
recorded, and it binds a commit.

One line of the `preflight` capture above is worth reading against that boundary rather than as a
result. This capture ran `preflight --work-order WO-MOK-019 --phase review`, naming the work order
on the command line. CI does not: it reads the work order out of the pull request's body trailer,
which still names `WO-MOK-012`, and the merge brought `master`'s own unrelated `WO-MOK-012` into
this tree. So CI's review preflight passes against that work order rather than this one, and its
green is not this capture's green. `README.md`'s *What is still owed* item 7 records it.
