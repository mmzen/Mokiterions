SPEC-MOK-002 rule 5 does not describe the engine's public interface, it closes it: "the library
target's public interface is exactly the union of the three lists below." Its 2026-08-20
amendment then says what the check on that closure is -- "VER-MOK-016's interface-growth check
compares the engine's public surface item for item against this table, so the check is re-run
against four rows rather than three."

A comparison against a closed list needs the surface as a list. `grep pub` does not produce one:
of the fifteen units of growth this work order makes, fourteen are enum variants, and a variant
carries no visibility keyword of its own. That is precisely how the defect the amendment's fourth
row corrects came about -- a field appended to a variant that was already public is invisible to
any check that looks for the word `pub`.

So this file carries the surface, extracted rather than grepped: section 2 is the complete
enumeration at the candidate, 186 entries, and section 3 is the baseline as a diff against it.


1. The reader, and why its enumeration cannot have omitted an item
------------------------------------------------------------------

analysis/interface.py takes the three files that make up the library target -- src/lib.rs and the
two modules it declares `pub mod` for -- and emits, for every public item, its declaration with
its body or value removed, and underneath it the field or variant list. Bodies, match arms,
comments and constant values are dropped on purpose: they are not surface, and a public function
that gains a match arm has not grown the interface.

    $ git show <rev>:mokiterions-core/src/lib.rs > lib.rs        (and cli.rs, simulation.rs)
    $ python analysis/interface.py <that directory>
    exit 0

cruder reader that cannot miss anything either:

5. What does not grow, clause by clause
----------------------------------------

The amendment makes "what does not grow" part of the enumeration, so each clause is a check.
All of them reduce to two diffs, taken over the listing:

    one line differs: EventType::ALL's length.        62 declaration entries each

    $ diff <(grep -E "^    pub " baseline) <(grep -E "^    pub " candidate)
    (no output)                                       43 public field entries each

- **`AgentSnapshot` still carries a `String`, a `Coordinate`, a `Territory`, four `u8`
  attributes and an `Option<Action>`.** Its eight public fields are among the 43 that are
  identical, so no fifth attribute exists and the suffered-attack record is not one of them.
- **`DecisionSnapshot` is unchanged in shape** and carries the seven new verbs through the
  `Action` it already holds -- `pub proposed: Action` and `pub applied: Option<Action>`, both
  unchanged, both now denoting eleven verbs rather than four.
- **The eleven types the amendment names as untouched** -- `WorldSnapshot`,
  `TerritorySnapshot`, `ResourceSnapshot`, `TickOutcome`, `Event`, `DecisionOutcome`,
  `RegenerationSkipReason`, `Coordinate`, `Territory`, `Direction` and `FoodClass` -- appear in
  the listing at both commits with identical declarations, identical field or variant lists and
  identical derive lists.
- **The first list's nine items and the five authorized additions are otherwise identical.**
  `Policy` is the one item of the nine that grew, by exactly the one variant row 1 of the growth
  table accounts for; the other eight, and all five additions, are unchanged in declaration and in
  members. The additions list is a ceiling rather than a checklist: two of the accessors it
  authorizes, population per territory and resource counts per territory by calorie class, are
  present at neither commit, and rule 5 requires that absence -- "an item on it that no relocated
  test requires must not be added". The engine states the same thing itself, in the comment above
  `impl RunSummary`.
- **No accessor is added and no method is added.** The 62 declaration entries include every
  public `fn` of the library target; one of the 62 differs, and it is a `const`.
- **No trait implementation on a public type is added or removed.** Thirteen at both commits,
  identical: `Default for Density`, and `fmt::Display` for the twelve types that print. The
  derive lists are in the listing beside their types for the same reason -- a derive on a public
  type is a public implementation, and removing one is a breaking change that no `pub` keyword
  records.
- **`EventType::ALL` is the only `pub const` whose declaration changed.** Four exist on the
  surface; the other three are `cli::USAGE`, `simulation::CELLS_PER_TERRITORY` and
  `simulation::Density::DEFAULT`, and all three declarations are unchanged. On `USAGE`'s value,

Rule 6 was re-checked on 2026-08-20 and not amended. Its clauses, measured on the listing:

- **The ten prohibited names stay private.** Seven of the ten appear nowhere in the listing at
  either commit. Three appear, and all three appear as private fields of `Simulation`:

        agents: Vec<Mokiterion>,
        foods: Vec<Food>,
        entropy: SplitMix64,

  A private field of a public struct is not reachable from outside the crate, and these three are
  the world's agents, its resources and its entropy state -- the first bullet's subjects. That
  they are named in the listing at all is the reader keeping private members rather than hiding
  them, so the claim can be read off the enumeration instead of trusted.
- **No `pub(crate)` item is widened**, because there is no restricted visibility to widen:

        $ grep -nE "pub\((crate|super|in )" lib.rs cli.rs simulation.rs | wc -l

- **No public item hands out a borrow of engine state, an iterator over its collections or a
  handle that permits mutation.** Every public `fn` in the listing returns an owned value, with
  one exception: `EventType::as_str(self) -> &'static str`, whose reference is into the program's
  own static text and not into a `Simulation`. `Simulation::run` and `execute` take `&mut W` on
  writers the caller owns. No `Iterator`, no `impl Trait`, no lifetime-parameterized return type
  and no reference-typed field appears anywhere on the surface.
- **No `&self` method mutates through interior mutability**, because no engine type can:

- **A target is an identifier, not a reference.** The seven `Action` variants carry

7. Two changes the surface diff cannot show, one of which is a wording defect
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

**`cli::USAGE`'s value changed, and SPEC-MOK-002's sentence about `pub const`s does not allow for
it.** The declaration is `pub const USAGE: &str` at both commits; the item behind it is not. The
figures are of the whole item, from `pub const USAGE` to the `);` that closes its `concat!`:

    baseline    1,937 bytes   32 lines   sha256 a53d76243575c25e…
    candidate   2,266 bytes   36 lines   sha256 7d5e9035bf0e697d…

The change is four lines describing the social policy, `social` added to the two `--policy` value
lists, and `social` added to the invalid-value diagnostic. It is obliged by an approved
requirement, `REQ-MOK-057`: "`--policy` accepts `social`, and its usage text, its long help and
its invalid-value diagnostic all name the four values." So the change is authorized and correct.

What is not exact is SPEC-MOK-002 rule 5's own sentence, at line 250: "`EventType::ALL`'s length
moving from `12` to `15` remains the only change to a `pub const`." Read of a constant's *type*
that is true, and it is the reading the enumeration checks -- `USAGE: &str` is unchanged, so
rule 5's list still closes. Read of a constant's *value* it is false, because `USAGE`'s value is
329 bytes longer. The amendment record at line 35 is exact where the rule text is not: it says
`ALL`'s length "is public-surface growth because that array is a `pub const`", which is a claim
about that array and not about every constant.

This is recorded here as a defect in an approved specification's wording, not corrected. Rule 5
is technical-owner text and its amendment is an owner act; nothing in this packet may edit it.
The correction the sentence needs is one clause -- "the only change to a `pub const`'s type" --
and it changes no requirement, no interface item and no test. Until it is taken, a verifier
running this check against the sentence as written will find a second changed `pub const` and be
right to.

**The three `EventType` variants were inserted rather than appended, and two consequences of that
are observable to a host without being interface growth.** `EventType` derives `PartialOrd, Ord`
at both commits, so its declaration order is a public total order, and it is a C-like enum, so a
host may cast it to an integer.

- The derived order between any two pre-existing variants is preserved: inserting in the middle
  moves no existing variant relative to another. A host that sorts events by kind sees the twelve
  old kinds in the same relative order, with three new kinds between `territory_crossed` and
  `simulation_ended`.
- The discriminants of the two variants after the insertion point do move: `SimulationEnded` from
  10 to 13 and `ActionTrace` from 11 to 14. Nothing in this workspace casts an `EventType` to an
  integer, and no specification assigns it a numeric value, so nothing depends on it here.

The observer does depend on `ALL`'s order, at `mokiterions-tui/src/state.rs:552`, where the type
filter cycles through it: the three new kinds now appear in that cycle between `territory_crossed`
and `simulation_ended`. That is the observer's obligation and is owed as observer.md, together
with the authority table's new rows; it is named here because the insertion point is what causes
it and this is where the insertion point is measured.

9. What this file does not establish
-------------------------------------

- Nothing here is a verification verdict. VER-MOK-016 is the contract, VREC-MOK-012 the record.
- The enumeration is of the engine's library target. The observer's public surface is not in it,
  and neither is the engine's binary target, which declares no item of its own.
- Surface identity is not behavioural identity. `EventDetail::event_type` and the `Display`
  implementations gained arms for the three new kinds; their signatures did not change, which is
  why they do not appear in section 3. What those arms print is post/reads.md and the test census,
  not this file.
- analysis/interface.py reads Rust with a brace counter, imported from analysis/regions.py so the
  two evidence readers cannot disagree about where an item ends. It does not resolve types, so it
  cannot tell that `Vec<(String, u8)>` names no private type -- section 6's name check is what
  establishes that, and it is a check on names rather than on resolution.
- Rule 6's first bullet is a claim about capability, and the strongest thing measured here is a
  property of every signature on the surface. A public item that returned an owned copy today and
  a borrow tomorrow would show in this listing; one that returns an owned copy of something it
  should not copy at all would not.
