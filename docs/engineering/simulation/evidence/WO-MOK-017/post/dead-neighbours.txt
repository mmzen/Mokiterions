2. What moved, and why the pinned scenario stopped working
---------------------------------------------------------
    pre-change  first death M05 at tick 604  interior     search settles on M05 at tick 604
    candidate   first death M01 at tick 314  NOT interior search settles on M11 at tick 399

    The first identifier to die is M01 at the candidate, and M01 is the lowest
    identifier in the list. No living identifier sorts below it, so BackTab from it wraps to
    the top rather than moving down, and `assert!(backward < dead)` cannot hold. The only way
    to keep a scenario pinned to the first death would have been to weaken that assertion to
    admit the wrap -- a different claim about a different behavior, and exactly what
    `SPEC-MOK-002` rule 12 forbids. Searching for the state rule 10.6 describes keeps both
    assertions as written.

    The search terminates at both commits well inside the 700-tick horizon the test
    declares: tick 604 pre-change on M05, tick 399 at the candidate on M11. The amendment
    asserts that rather than assuming it: if the run finishes with no such state, the test
    fails saying so instead of falling out of the loop having proved nothing.

3. Why the scenario cannot simply take the baseline policy
---------------------------------------------------------
The comment's other claim -- that baseline cannot serve because it starves the whole
population at once -- is measured here too, since it is what rules out the cheapest fix.
    pre-change  12 of 12 identifiers die on tick 119
    candidate   12 of 12 identifiers die on tick 119

    Rule 4 applies no waste condition, so the baseline population starves together and the
    tick that produces a dead selection produces no living one. That is also why these two
    rows are identical: `INT-MOK-010` promises byte-identity for `--policy baseline`, and
    `post/byte-identity.txt` measures the promise kept across all thirty baseline cells.

4. What this does not claim
---------------------------
  * Not that the identifier is stable. It is not, and that is the point: the amended test
    searches for the state rather than naming the Mokiterion, so the next change to the
    world moves the subject without touching the test.
  * Not that a live run is the right way to reach rule 10.6's state. Constructing a dead
    selection with living neighbours directly would be a scenario coupled to nothing, and it
    would not need a 700-tick run to find one. This test discharges two cases in
    `VER-MOK-005` -- `REQ-MOK-021`'s "Selected Mokiterion dies" and `REQ-MOK-023`'s
    "Selection cycles living Mokiterions only" -- and neither states how the state is
    reached, so the search is inside the scenario as written. Changing it to a constructed
    one would be a `VER-MOK-005` amendment this work order has no authority to make; raised
    in `manual-assessment.md`.
  * Not that the deaths themselves are correct. Which identifiers die and when is
    `post/survivors.txt` and `post/divergence.txt`, not this file.

RESULT: PASS -- the first identifier to die was interior before the change and is the

search for rule 10.6's state terminates at both commits, and both assertions stand as
