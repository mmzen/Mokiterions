the waste condition of rule 5 and rule 19 and nothing else, and the reason is `INT-MOK-010`:

---------------------

record is parsed. `pre/pre-manifest.txt` and `post/post-manifest.txt` carry the digests, and
they are the weaker of the two statements.

3. What identity here does and does not prove
---------------------------------------------
It proves that no observable behavior of the `baseline` source moved: not a proposal, not a
draw, not a regeneration, not a death, not the final summary, anywhere in the declared matrix.
Because all four sources share one world, one regeneration rule and one entropy stream, it also
constrains everything shared. Rules 14 to 16, rule 9's eat effect and rule 6's food table all
run under `baseline` too, so a change to any of them would appear here.
`post/world-rules-unchanged.txt` makes the same point by reading the source; this is its
behavioral half, and neither substitutes for the other.

It proves it only as far as `baseline` runs, and that is a real bound rather than a caveat.
`baseline` proposes without regard to the world, so it starves:

    the tick every baseline run ended at              119 to 193, of a possible 1,000
    how they ended                                    30 by extinction
    consumptions across all 30 baseline cells         34
    the tick the obligated runs reached, for contrast 359 to 1000

So the identity above constrains the shared world over the first 193 ticks at most,
and over the later ticks of a thousand-tick obligated run it constrains nothing. It could not be
otherwise: a control that ignores the world cannot survive in it, which is exactly why
`INT-MOK-010` promises `baseline` byte-identity rather than `baseline` survival. The later ticks
are covered instead by reading the source -- `post/world-rules-unchanged.txt` -- and by the draw
accounting in `post/entropy.txt`. Anyone reading this file as "the world is unchanged for a
thousand ticks" is reading more than the measurement carries.

It does not prove that the other three sources diverge only where they should, which is
`divergence.txt`. Nor does it prove the entropy stream is consumed identically in the obligated
runs -- it is not, and legitimately so: a wider candidate set draws a different number of
times, which is `post/entropy.txt` and route R2 of `divergence.txt`.

RESULT: PASS -- 30 of 30 baseline cells byte-identical, 30 of 30 exit codes equal.
