1. Every changed engine line, and the one place they all sit
------------------------------------------------------------
This is the general claim, and it is made first because the four named rules are corollaries
of it. The two engine texts are aligned by `difflib.SequenceMatcher` and every non-equal
block is listed. Each is then tested for containment in the non-waste condition family.

    pre-change text would mean the two sources are not the two commits they claim to be.

    inserted immediately above `fits`. It is also tested on both sides, so a world rule
    deleted inside a `replace` block cannot pass by having an innocent candidate range.

3. Rule 9: what consuming a resource does
-----------------------------------------
`REQ-MOK-060` changes whether a resource is eaten and never what eating restores. The clip is
still here and still unconditional -- `saturating_add` then `min(ATTRIBUTE_MAX)`, on satiety
and on energy alike, printed below inside the identical region. That is the whole relationship
between this rule and the correction: the corrected condition decides that a resource whose
restoration will be clipped is worth taking anyway, and this code goes on clipping it. The
waste `REQ-MOK-060` tolerates is real waste, not waste the effect was quietly changed to
avoid. Nothing here needed to change for the correction to work, and nothing did.

4. The food table
-----------------
This table is the input to the corrected allowance. `R * R / 100` is `2`, `9` and `25` for the
three classes because `R` is `15`, `30` and `50` here, so the specification's boundaries of
`87`, `79` and `75` are derived from these six numbers. Had the table moved, the engine and
the specification could agree while both were wrong.

5. Rules 14 to 16: regeneration
-------------------------------
The composition curve in `post/composition.txt` is the joint product of what regenerates and
what is eaten, and `REQ-MOK-060` is the claim that consumption alone explains the drift. That
claim is only testable with regeneration held fixed -- including rule 16's uniform class
selection, which is what would otherwise be suspected of favouring high class.

6. What this comparison does not settle
---------------------------------------
  * That the engine changed in one place is not that the change is right. The condition's own
    correctness is `post/divergence.txt` and the suite's new boundary test, not this file.
  * The regions above shift by a line count between the two texts, because the condition
    family gained lines ahead of them. Shifted is not changed, and the digests are computed
    on the text rather than on the line numbers, so a pure move reads as identical here -- as
    it should.
  * The test module is excluded throughout. Its changes are real and are reconciled name by
    name in `post/test-census-reconciliation.md`, with the two consequential TUI fixes in
    `post/updated-tests.md`.
  * Two crates outside this module changed and are not compared here: `mokiterions-tui`'s
    state and render tests, both of which are test-side and both accounted for in
    `post/updated-tests.md`. No `mokiterions-tui` source rule is a world rule.

RESULT: PASS -- every changed line in the engine lies inside the three functions of the
non-waste condition, and rule 4, rule 9's eat effect, the food table and rules 14 to 16
are identical between the two commits, character for character.
